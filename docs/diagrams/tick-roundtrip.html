Runtime · udp-realtime-gameserver

One tick, end to end

Input arrives as a 7-byte datagram, the room is simulated on a fixed 30 Hz tick, and a snapshot of 74 bytes goes back out. Everything interesting is in the two things a UDP server cannot avoid: a packet that never arrives, and a client that has already moved on.

measured with cmd/botswarm on one machine · tick budget 33.3 ms · snapshot 74 B for a ten-player room
The honest part

These are numbers from one machine and one run, not a capacity claim. The useful comparison is the tick percentiles against the budget: p99 at 19.7 ms of 33.3 ms means the loop has room, and p50 at 5.0 ms means the room is mostly waiting for the clock.

Why quantise positions

An int16 centimetre is 74 bytes where a float pair would be more, and at 30 Hz broadcast to every player in a room that difference is the bandwidth budget. The precision is a decision: centimetres, not millimetres.
